# Electrochemical science workbench

Open an experiment or multi-instrument project and choose **Electrochemical science**. Select the analysis, its measurement tables, and the relevant columns and experimental settings. Each calculation writes its result tables and a methods record under `Workbench/Science` in the configured workspace. The methods record and analysis history identify the input paths, selected columns, instrument metadata, and supplied model parameters.

## Measurement conventions

- Column headers must identify the measured quantity and its supported simple unit. Common examples include `potential_V`, `Ewe/mV`, `current_A`, `<I>/mA`, `time_s`, `frequency_Hz`, `Zreal_ohm`, and `Zimag_ohm`. The workbench converts supported prefixes. Compound units such as A/kg, mA/s, and mA/cm² are incompatible with measured total current.
- The CV tools split reversals in acquisition order and share the turning sample between adjacent sweeps. Measured charge includes all samples at either edge of the closed potential window. A hold at a reversal belongs to the sweep that arrived at the turning potential. Isolate the intended sweeps when a stepped or noisy waveform creates extra reversals.
- Potential comparisons across selected traces use a common reference. When recorded references agree, the workbench uses that scale. If a reference is missing, declare the common scale. If a recorded scale differs, enter a reviewed offset in volts to add to that trace. Use the resolved scale for analysis inputs such as a common bias or formal potential. The methods record identifies the declarations, applied offsets, and resolved scale.
- Enter scan rate in V/s, area in cm², concentration in mol/cm³, diffusion coefficient and kinematic viscosity in cm²/s, and absolute temperatures in Kelvin unless the Arrhenius control explicitly selects Celsius. Time windows select measured samples for fits and stability diagnostics. Charge integration linearly interpolates window crossings and current zero crossings. Its separate anodic and cathodic integrals retain their measured signs. A CV without time requires a supplied constant scan rate for the selected sweep.

## Voltammetry and area

Peak tracking reports signed sampled extrema by sweep. The chosen extremum must be unique, interior to the selected samples, and have the selected polarity. Set a potential window to isolate overlapping features. Correct backgrounds in the supplied data when required. Scan-rate regression uses the chosen sweep and records the linear, square-root, or log₁₀ rate transformation, residuals, and fitted intercept.

At a user-selected nonfaradaic bias, the capacitance analysis regresses `(i_increasing − i_decreasing)/2` against scan rate. The slope has units of farads. ECSA divides an independently supplied capacitance or charge magnitude by the matching material-specific value per cm². Use a reference determined under relevant surface and electrolyte conditions.

The reversible Randles–Ševčík analysis estimates diffusion coefficient from the slope of `|i_peak|` against `√v` using `|i_peak| = 0.4463 n F A c √(n F D v / (R T))`. It applies to a reversible process with planar semi-infinite diffusion. The measured series must represent the same electrode and bulk concentration and use a corrected background. [Randles, 1948](https://doi.org/10.1039/TF9484400327) · [Ševčík, 1948](https://doi.org/10.1135/cccc19480349) · [Experimental validation and limits](https://doi.org/10.1021/acselectrochem.5c00457)

The high-rate Laviron analysis fits the anodic and cathodic peak potentials against `ln(v)` for a diffusionless couple. With cathodic transfer coefficient `α`, signed slopes are `RT/((1−α)nF)` and `−RT/(αnF)`. Given the supplied formal potential, each branch slope and intercept yield a conditional standard rate constant in s⁻¹. The workbench screens out scans with a peak less than `2RT/nF` from the formal potential or with peak separation below `0.200 V/n`. It rejects branch estimates that disagree beyond 0.15 in `α` or a factor of three in rate constant. Those screens are workbench checks. Applicability still depends on a diffusionless experiment, high-rate asymptotic behavior, an independently determined formal potential, and control of ohmic distortion. [Laviron, 1979](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-0728(79)80075-3)

## Transients and reaction charge

Chronoamperometry compares the Cottrell-plus-offset form `I(t) = a/√(t−t_step) + b` to a single exponential plus offset over identical selected samples. The exponential amplitude is referenced to the first fitted sample. The result tables provide fitted values, residuals, RMSE and AICc with the fitted residual variance counted as a parameter. Fit intervals must begin after the potential step. Cottrell is conditional on the planar semi-infinite diffusion regime. [Cottrell, 1903](https://doi.org/10.1515/zpch-1903-4229)

Scharifker–Hills compares `[(I−I_background)/(I_peak−I_background)]²` against the instantaneous and progressive growth curves at the measured `(t−t_step)/(t_peak−t_step)`. It requires a known step time, an appropriate polarity after optional constant background subtraction, and a resolved interior peak with a rise and decay. RMSE summarizes normalized shape agreement. Interfacial charging, competing reactions, overlapping peaks and non-diffusion-limited growth can invalidate comparisons. [Scharifker and Hills, 1983, equations 20 and 24](https://doi.org/10.1016/0013-4686(83)85163-9)

Chronopotentiometry reports a candidate transition at the unique interior maximum of `|dE/dt|` above the supplied threshold in the chosen interval. Exclude switching transients. OCP stability fits linear drift within the selected time window and reports signed drift plus residual noise standard deviation with `n−2` degrees of freedom. Optional user limits evaluate both absolute drift and detrended noise.

Deposition takes user-supplied mole fractions `xᵢ`, formula-unit molar masses `Mᵢ` and electron counts `nᵢ`. Given reaction charge magnitude `Q` and deposition efficiency fraction `η`, total deposited moles are `ηQ/(F Σxᵢnᵢ)`. Component masses multiply this value by `xᵢMᵢ`. Independent composition or purity measurements are needed to support the indicated composition.

Faradaic efficiency for each product is `100 nᵢ F Nᵢ / Q`, with product amount `Nᵢ` in moles. Mass inputs are converted using the supplied molar mass. Choose charge in the declared polarity or signed net charge and use product amounts from the same interval. A sum over 100% is flagged for review and retained for troubleshooting.

## Polarization, impedance and rotation series

Tafel sensitivity compares explicit overpotential windows on the selected current branch using the desktop classical linear Tafel calculation. The fit uses `E_measured − iR_u − E_eq` and `log₁₀(|i|/A)`. Corrosion series use the desktop analysis for Ecorr candidates, Icorr, branch slopes and ambiguity flags. Corrosion rate additionally requires geometric area, equivalent weight and density.

Mott–Schottky by frequency uses the existing potential-resolved EIS analysis to obtain corrected storage capacitance. It runs the strict bias-window diagnostic on each measured frequency of a complete potential–frequency grid and screens slope-sign agreement. On dense grids the search considers longer contiguous windows to meet the minimum sample count and voltage span. Candidate intercepts require independent semiconductor context. Rejected windows retain diagnostic status and suppress the reported flat-band candidate. Frequency selection uses exact measured values.

Arrhenius estimates apparent activation energy from the slope of `ln(k)` against reciprocal Kelvin temperature with `Ea = −R × slope`. Provide a comparable positive kinetic observable for each selected run in one common unit. The sign of an apparent activation energy is retained.

The rotating-disk analysis interpolates signed current at one shared bias and uses the declared polarity to obtain its magnitude. It converts speed using `ω = 2π rpm/60`. The Levich coefficient is `B = 0.62 n F A c D^(2/3) ν^(−1/6)`. Levich fits `|i|` against `√ω` in a transport-limited regime. Koutecky–Levich fits `1/|i|` against `1/√ω` under additive transport and kinetic resistance. In both cases the fit estimates an apparent electron count given the supplied physical constants. Koutecky–Levich returns a finite kinetic current only when the positive intercept is resolved from zero. [Levich, *Physicochemical Hydrodynamics*, 1962](https://books.google.com/books?id=DAtRAAAAMAAJ) · [Koutecký and Levich, 1957](https://www.mathnet.ru/php/archive.phtml?jrnid=dan&option_lang=eng&paperid=22530&wshow=paper)
